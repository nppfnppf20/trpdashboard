Site context
Site description
The assessment site is comprised of two parcels of land, approximately 400m apart. Both are comprised of medium to large agricultural fields, in arable use and for animal grazing. There are scattered trees across the site and existing hedgerow at field boundaries. The settlement of Holt is approximately directly south of the southern parcel.

Planning discussion
Benefits of proposal
Significant sustainability benefits include:
- Support the UK's goal of net-zero carbon by 2050.
- Facilitate the transition to a zero-carbon energy system by generating renewable energy.
- Significantly increase the biodiversity of the site.
- Enable the continuation of grazing of livestock throughout the operational period.

Solar farm will contribute significantly towards social sustainability by indirectly providing low cost, clean power to businesses, contributing to lower business rates and employing local people.

Landscape and visual effects
Northern Parcel - The site slopes gently upwards towards the northwest. While there will inevitably be residential receptors with view onto the site, the parcel does benefit from strong screening from treeline on all sides, and no immediate residential receptors on its boundaries, with the nearest being approximately 500m away. A Public Right of Way (PRoW) crosses the southern section of the site, while another passes along the northeastern boundary. A number of PRoWs in close proximity to the site are likely to experience views across the site.

Southern Parcel - The site slopes gently upwards towards both the northwest and the southwest, with a slight depression towards the centre. While this parcel does benefit from a good level of screening from tree line on all sides, there are far more potentially sensitive receptors, with a number of residential properties within 100m of the site boundary, although again, due to the site's topography and intervening vegetation, these may be well screened. A number of PRoWs also cross the site.

A site visit will be necessary to get an accurate view of the landscape and visual setting of both parcels. There will be some inevitable effects upon local landscape character as a result of any proposed development, especially one of the scale suggested. However, with the benefit of appropriate mitigation, those effects have the potential to be reduced in the longer term and would be localised and limited in nature.

Due to the location of various residential properties / settlements, PRoWs and road receptors, even with mitigation, there will be some residual effects on the surrounding area – an LVIA will confirm whether any of these are of relevance in planning terms.

Ecology and Arboriculture
There are no nationally significant ecological designations on either parcel. There are locally designated County Wildlife Sites close to the borders of both sites. Granted European Species Applications indicate that there may be the presence of bats around both parcels. The closest ecological SSSI is the Inwood, Warleigh SSSI approximately 5km west of the site. Given the distance, this is highly unlikely to pose any significant planning risk.

There are a number of trees within and along boundaries of both parcels, although none are subject to Tree Preservation Orders. An Arboricultural Impact Assessment will further inform this. Given the fact that the site is large and agricultural land can form good habitat for some notable protected species, as well as the presence of wooded areas and ponds on site, it is recommended that a Preliminary Ecological Assessment is commissioned at an early stage.

Site Access
Northern Parcel - As the site is bounded on all sides by tree line and hedgerow, and surrounded by agricultural land, access opportunities are limited. The most suitable access point appears to be to the east of the site off 'The St'. This route involves a long track alongside what appear to be agricultural buildings, before crossing an agricultural field. If this land is not within the ownership boundary for the site, third party consent will be necessary.

Southern Parcel - Access to the site looks to be fairly constrained. The most logical access point appears to be at the north of the site from Copse Farm. This access point is reached via a long, winding, single-track country lane which runs past the entrance to the National Trust Great Chalfield Manor.

On the whole, access appears to be rather constrained for both sites. Third party consent may be required and the practical implications of moving HGVs along narrow country lanes will need to be carefully considered with advice from a transport consultant.

Heritage and archaeology
Northern Parcel - The Broughton Gifford conservation area is approximately 500m east of the site. There is a cluster of listed buildings approximately 380m west of the site. The majority of these are Grade II but include the Grade I listed National Trust property Great Chalfield Manor. The Great Chalfield Manor Registered Park and Garden runs north from here, parallel with the western boundary of the site, approximately 500m west.

Southern Parcel - The Holt Conservation area lies on the sites southern boundary, at points only 70m away. This does present a significant planning risk. Careful siting, design and mitigation will be necessary to limit adverse impact on the setting of this conservation area. 3 Grade II listed buildings are located approximately 320m west of the site. Great Chalfield Manor and its associated buildings are 420m north of the site, but do appear to benefit from strong screening from an intervening area of woodland.

There are a significant number of archaeological points of interest on and around both parcels. These will need to be assessed by a heritage expert.

Agricultural land grade and use
National and Local policy does not preclude development on BMV land, but states that preference should be given to sites which do not constitute BMV land. It is therefore recommended that an intrusive site survey is undertaken at an early stage to determine the grading of the site. If, as expected, the site is found to contain BMV land, then potentially suitable sites in the surrounding area should be examined to demonstrate that there are no deliverable sites of lower grade in the area. It is also recommended that an independent Agricultural Land Use Statement is instructed to provide robust and independent conclusions.

Broadly, desk-based research shows that the sites are in an area of Grade 3 agricultural land. While this is largely an indicative grading, more detailed information is available for the southern parcel specifically, as previous surveys suggest it is comprised Grade 3a agricultural land.

Flood risk and drainage
The entire of the southern parcel is within Flood Zone 1. A small section of the northern parcel, along its southern boundary, appears to be in Flood Zone 3. In terms of surface water flooding, the vast majority of the site is designated as being very low risk, with patches of medium and high risk. Although these patches are not large enough to present a showstopping constraint, it is advised that water-sensitive equipment be located away from these areas. As the site is over 1ha in size, a Flood Risk Assessment will be required.

Summary and recommendation
The sites are considered to be suitable for the development of a solar scheme but do have some potentially significant risks and so should be subject to further advice and assessment on: impact on setting of heritage assets, visual impact on landscape, ALC, interaction with PRoWs crossing the site, and access. Careful siting, design and mitigation will be important. Both sites do appear to benefit from strong existing screening and a relatively flat topography.

The size of the scheme means that landscape and visual matters will be important – some parts are relatively well screened or can be screened while others are open and harder to screen. It is not subject to any other significant planning constraints, in our assessment, other than the potential need for breeding bird and other ecological surveys which may affect programme and potentially site extent if offsite mitigation is required.

Contacts of TRP who have worked on nearby solar schemes in recent years have reported that their scheme was deemed to be EIA, and that Wiltshire Council also required a relatively onerous cumulative assessment of solar sites within a 10km radius of their site, suggesting that a positive EIA screening may be likely for this site, which would have a significant impact on programme and budget. An early EIA screening opinion request is recommended to establish the LPA's position in terms of EIA.

Development offsets and additional mitigation planting are highly likely to be required where development interacts closely with the PRoW and with any residential receptors. A Biodiversity Net Gain of greater than 10% will need to be demonstrated through a specialist report and plan.
