Preliminary Feasibility Note – Land at Fitcher Brook, Green Street, Hallow WR2 6PX
Thank you for sending through the above site for an initial high-level appraisal.
This letter summarises the main, high-level planning related constraints and opportunities for the
site shown below for the purpose of understanding the likely scope of a future planning application
for a solar project.
Further detailed work will be needed, including a site visit, to determine the position of the
development within the site and to understand more of the sizing considerations and any
constraints. This will form part of our first stage of work, if appointed.

In summary, the majority of the land appears to have good to very good potential for hosting
development of this type from a high-level appraisal perspective. There do not appear to be any
showstopping designations, however some parts of the south and east of the site are constrained in
terms of flooding designations. Subject to development avoiding these areas, the only other
significant constraint involves potential impacts to relevant landscape receptors including the
numerous Public Rights of Way which run through and close to the site, and residential receptors in
the form of isolated farmhouses and cottages. Especially of relevance in this regard is the Grade II
listed Greenstreets Farmhouse which falls within the site. Careful design and siting will be required
and it is probable that some fields or parts of fields will need to be left free of development, e.g. to
minimise risk to amenity or objection from properties, harm to the significance of heritage assets,
PRoW and areas at risk of flooding.
Further assessment will be needed in relation to these specific potential constraints; including
flooding, impact on PRoWs and residential receptors, general landscape and visual impact
considerations, views from / to heritage assets, ecology, landscape, trees and noise. Given that a
number of the parcels would have to be accessed directly from single track roads by construction
traffic, this will also merit further specialist consideration. Specialist advice will be needed in respect
of the key matters identified and to consider cumulative impacts.

Planning designations
None of the land is located within Green Belt. None of the land is
included within Conservation Areas.
Some parcels are variously subject to flooding (rivers), flooding
(surface water), heritage and ecology designations. Numerous
Public Rights of Way run through the site. This is elaborated
upon in the sections below.
Constraints and Opportunities
Landscape and visual
Open, rolling landform with site levels which are broadly
comparable with natural height variations within the wider
landscape. Generally fairly flat. The fields represent significantly
sized parcels and all appear to be agricultural land. Fields tend to
be bounded by hedgerows and mature trees are common
features of the landscape.
The site falls within the local landscape character type ‘Principal
Timbered Farmlands’ as defined by the Worcestershire County
Council Landscape Character Assessment (2012).
Numerous Public Rights of Way run through the land, and the
land is visible from additional PRoWs located in the surrounding
countryside. The Worcestershire CC PRoW map extract below
shows footpaths (purple dashed line) and bridleways (solid green
line).
Buffering and mitigation planting will be needed and, if space
allows, avoiding development on both sides of a PRoW.
Ecology
The parcels are generally not subject to any specific ecological
designations, however Laughern Brook which runs along the
southern boundary of the land is a ‘Site of Regional or Local
Wildlife Importance’.
Treelines, hedgerows and small wooded areas are visible within
the site on aerial imagery and OS Mapping.
Early input from an ecologist would therefore be advisable.
This site could likely benefit from nature enhancement through
additional planting along exposed boundaries and an additional
area / areas for biodiversity net gain.
Heritage
There is one listed building on the land (the Grade II listed
Greenstreet Farmhouse). The land itself is subject to no other
heritage designations.
A cluster of three Grade II listed buildings (Hill Farmhouse and
two associated buildings) are located approximately 300 metres
to the west of the land’s western boundary. The farmhouse itself
appears to be quite well screened from the site by other
agricultural buildings which comprise part of the Hill Farm
complex. Specialist input is required to determine the level of
harm and size of buffer needed.
There are also listed buildings in Shoulton and WIchenford but it
is not considered that the site would fall within their setting.
Flood risk
River flooding
Some parts of the south-eastern and south-western parcels are
within Flood Zones 2 and 3 as the map extract below shows and,
subject to specialist advice, development within these areas may
be constrained particualrly if some comprise functional
floodplain. The northern parcel has no river flooding constraints.
Surface water flooding
The surface water flooding constraints are generally similar to
the river flooding constraints. There are some additional areas of
low/moderate surface water flooding potential towards the
north of the site and around the smaller streams, however these
areas are generally small as the map extract below shows.
Amenity
There are a limited number of residential properties in the area,
and those which are present are generally fairly isolated
farmhouses or cottages. Several residential dwellings appear to
be present within the red line – Greenstreets Farmhouse, Moody
Cow Cottage (which also forms part of the Greenstreets farm
complex) and Greenstreet Cottages.
The number of direct receptors therefore appears to be
relatively low. A site visit and specialist initial landscape advice
are recommended to analyse the land and its surroundings with
regard to amenity issues.
To minimise the amenity risk, but also the level of objection we
recommend separation from nearby residential properties and
adequate mitigation to avoid demonstrable harm to their
amenity.
Agricultural land
The land is broadly considered to be Grade 3 (green - good /
moderate) agricultural land as the map extract below shows. An
ALC survey should be commissioned at the application stage to
assess the quality of the land in more detail.
The land is considered to have a moderate likelihood of being
BMV land (pink).
Other
There are no airfields or airstrips nearby that would be potential
receptors in terms of glint / glare. The B204 is the most relevant
potential receptor in this regard but it is generally screened from
the site by trees and hedgerows.
Access would appear to be possible to all parcels from nearby
single track roads which tend to connect to the B4204. Although
very narrow and passing places would need to be created,
original advice relating to Birchall 1 from Apex indicated that
access could be achieved. Consideration would also need to be
given to whether Fitcher Brook could be crossed or whether a
second access from the south would also be necessary.
