Heritage
Policy Framework

Policy DC2 of the Warrington Local Plan sets out the Council's approach to the historic environment. It requires that development proposals conserve, and where appropriate enhance, the significance of both designated and non-designated heritage assets. Where proposals would lead to less than substantial harm to a designated asset, the harm must be weighed against the public benefits of the scheme. For non-designated heritage assets and archaeological remains, the policy requires that significance is conserved in a manner commensurate with the importance of the remains, with preservation in situ the preferred approach where development affecting archaeological sites is otherwise acceptable. All applications affecting a heritage asset must be accompanied by a Statement of Significance and, where necessary, a Heritage Impact Assessment.

Policy ENV7 of the Warrington Local Plan establishes that proposals for low carbon or renewable energy development will be permitted provided that they satisfy the requirements of other relevant Plan policies and would not result in unacceptable harm to the local environment. In assessing such proposals, the Council will have regard to the environmental, social and economic benefits that the proposals would provide.
Assessment

A Heritage Statement has been prepared in direct compliance with the requirements of Policy DC2, providing the information necessary to assess the impact of the proposals on heritage assets and their settings. The document constitutes the Statement of Significance required by the policy and has informed the design and layout of the scheme accordingly.

The Heritage Statement confirms that a geophysical survey has been undertaken across the site and that no significant archaeological anomalies were identified within the application boundary. This finding substantially reduces the likelihood of below-ground heritage assets of significance being present. Furthermore, the assessment concludes that the pre-medieval archaeological potential of the site is very low, providing further confidence that the proposals would not disturb remains of national or local importance.

With respect to the settings of designated and non-designated heritage assets, the Heritage Statement confirms that the proposals would not result in harm to the settings of any identified heritage asset. The scheme has therefore been assessed as generating no adverse heritage impact in this regard, satisfying the requirements of Policy DC2 in relation to the assessment of setting effects.

The existing hedgerow network within and surrounding the site is assumed to be retained as part of the proposed development. This approach is considered beneficial from a heritage perspective, as established hedgerows may form historic boundaries of potential significance. Their retention is consistent with the requirements of Policy DC2, which seeks to conserve those elements that contribute to the significance of the historic environment, including landscape features of historic interest.

Should any unforeseen archaeological remains be encountered during the course of development, it is acknowledged that further archaeological investigation may be secured by way of a planning condition. This approach accords with the provisions of Policy DC2, which allows for mitigation through excavation and recording as an alternative to in situ preservation where the latter is not justified, with findings to be deposited with the Historic Environment Record.
Conclusion

Subject to the imposition of an appropriate planning condition securing further archaeological investigation should previously unidentified remains be encountered during development, the proposals are considered to comply with Policy DC2 and Policy ENV7.
Landscape

Policy GB1 of the Warrington Local Plan sets out the general principles governing development within the Green Belt. The policy confirms that the Council will maintain the general extent of the Green Belt throughout the plan period and to at least 2050, and that the Council will plan positively to enhance the beneficial use of the Green Belt as part of Warrington's Green Infrastructure Network. Where development is proposed within the Green Belt, the policy requires that inappropriate development will not be permitted except in very special circumstances, and that any development must meet other relevant Local Plan policies and Supplementary Planning Documents. The policy also requires that a scheme of compensatory improvements to the environmental quality and accessibility of land remaining in the Green Belt be provided in association with development proposals.

The application site falls within the Green Belt as defined on the Local Plan Policies Map. The landscape character of the Green Belt in this location is therefore a material consideration of some significance. The proposal has been designed with sensitivity to its Green Belt context, and the assessment of landscape effects has informed both the layout and the mitigation strategy from the outset. The design approach seeks to retain and, where possible, enhance the existing landscape framework, including boundary vegetation and field patterns that contribute to the openness and character of the Green Belt.

In addition to the site-specific design response, Policy GB1 explicitly anticipates that the Council will plan positively to enhance the beneficial use of the Green Belt as part of the wider Green Infrastructure Network. The proposal incorporates a scheme of compensatory improvements to the environmental quality and accessibility of the land, consistent with the requirements of paragraph 11 of the policy. These measures have been identified and documented within the supporting technical evidence submitted with this application, which demonstrates that the landscape impacts of the development can be appropriately managed and mitigated, and that net benefits to the Green Belt's environmental quality can be secured through the measures proposed.

Subject to the implementation of the compensatory improvements and landscape mitigation measures described above, the proposals are considered to comply with Policy GB1.
