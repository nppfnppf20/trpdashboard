1.0 Executive Summary
This Planning Statement is submitted in support of a planning application to Warrington Borough
Council. The application is made by Renewable Connections Developments Ltd.
The application is for:
“The construction and operation of a ground mounted solar farm with associated infrastructure”
The site address is Land at Fowl Farm, Cuerdley, Warrington, WA5 2XQ. This includes land for
landscaping and biodiversity measures.
The proposal will be a temporary use and operational for 40 years. During that time, the total
generation is approximately 27,092 Megawatt hours (MWh) of clean renewable energy each year.
This will be enough to power in excess of 10,660 homes and offset over 4,930 tonnes of CO2 per
year.
The various aspects of the proposed development are described in this report providing details
where appropriate and available on technical aspects of equipment.
This Statement sets out the planning justification for the scheme, concluding that the proposal is in
accordance with the renewable energy policies of Warrington Borough Council‘s Local Plan and the
National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). The other material planning benefits of the proposal,
which should be weighed in support of the proposal in the planning balance, are also set out in the
statement.

2.0 Introduction
This Planning, Design & Access Statement (PDAS) has been prepared by Third Revolution Projects Ltd
on behalf of Renewable Connections Developments Ltd. (“the applicant”). It accompanies an
application to Warrington Borough Council (WBC) for full planning permission for:
“The construction and operation of a ground mounted solar farm with associated infrastructure”
The proposed solar farm will have a capacity of up to 27.15MW using solar panels fixed to the
ground via metal piles and supporting infrastructure.
The application site is located at Land at Fowl Farm, Cuerdley, Warrington, WA5 2XQ as shown on
the Development Zones Layout Plan (Dwg No: RCN1023-120) illustrated in Figure 1 below.
The scheme will enable the generation of approximately 27,092 MWh (Megawatt hours) of clean
renewable energy each year. This is enough to power the equivalent of 10,660 homes per year.
The site will enable continued agriculture in the form of grazing of small livestock, such as sheep,
while also considerably enhancing biodiversity by switching from intensive farming to meadow and
grazing land.
The scheme will be operational for up to 40 years. At the end of the operational lifespan, the solar
panels and associated infrastructure will be removed, and the land returned to its former use. The
landscape and biodiversity enhancements introduced through this proposal can remain and will be
compatible with continued agricultural use.

3.0 Site Surroundings and Planning History
3.1 Site and surroundings
The site is approximately 33 hectares of farmland part of a larger holding known as ‘Fowl Farm’ and
located to the west of Warrington. It is comprised of arable fields that are currently in agricultural
use. The landscape is generally flat across the site and the wider area. Fields are generally large in
scale, with established field boundaries comprising native hedgerows, ditches and scattered trees.
There are several farmsteads and dwellings associated with the current farm use within the vicinity
but these do not form part of the application site.
To the north of the site it is predominately open farmland with scattered commercial and residential
buildings. The A5080 and a railway line run immediately to the north. To the east is also open
farmland, with the outer edges of Penketh approximately 800m away. To the south is also open
farmland, however the A562 runs to the south and the Fiddlers Ferry Power station lies beyond.
Electricity pylons across the site and surrounding area, as well as the former Fiddlers Ferry Power
station contribute significantly to the industrialised context of the wider landscape. Immediately to
the west of the site is the outer urban fringe of Widness separated by the Sunnybank Woodland Park
and vegetated boundary of the site. The site will be accessed via a newly created access track from
Mowcroft Lane.
The site is subject to the following national and local level planning designations:
▪ The site is entirely within Flood Zone 1 (low risk)
▪ The site is within the Warrington Green Belt
▪ A PRoW (00102/1) runs directly north to south across the eastern section of the site
▪ Mineral Safeguarding area – Sand/Gravel
▪ The site is identified as part of a historic landfill buffer – Barrows Green Lane
Nearby designations include:
▪ Land at the western boundary of the site is a designated Core Biodiversity Area
▪ There are no Scheduled Monuments or Registered Parks or Gardens within the site’s boundaries.
However, a WWII anti-aircraft gun which is a scheduled monument is located approximately
270m north of the site. The closest nationally listed building is the Grade II listed Brookside
Farmhouse approximately 1km east of the site.
Land immediately to the northwest of the site (Boundary Farm and Abbey Farm, South Lane) is
within Halton Borough Council’s administrative boundary. Although this land is outside WBC, we are
aware that the Halton Delivery and Allocations Local Plan (DALP) (2022) has allocated parts of this
existing farmland for residential development.

4.0 The Proposed Development
The proposed development is for the installation and operation of a ground-mounted solar farm of
up to 27.15MW that will generate and deliver electrical power to the local distribution network.
The site will be designed to enable continued agricultural use in the form of grazing of animals such
as sheep. The existing agricultural use is thus not being lost either permanently or temporarily.
The scheme will be operational for up to 40 years. The applicant seeks a three-year period by which
to commence the development i.e. start the installation works. Once decommissioned, the
development will be completely removed and returned to its current use. A construction and
decommissioning plan can be prepared via a planning condition, should this be required. The various
components of the proposed development are set out below and shown on the accompanying
plans. As previously discussed, the development is of a temporary nature and the various aspects of
this temporary installation are described below.
The following descriptions are based on a solar farm design and technical equipment that could
viably be built today. The exact capacity and quantities of equipment may change post
determination, depending on the availability of equipment. Arrays have been designed using a solar
panel type that could reasonably be used to construct the development today. However, the final
choice of panel will be made prior to construction and will depend on availability and other factors.
This will affect the layout of the arrays and final number of panels used.
4.1 Solar arrays
A solar farm consists of solar PV panels organised into arrays alongside ancillary infrastructure. Most
of the site will remain open as grassed spacing between rows and field margins. The solar panels will
be mounted on metal frames set into the ground as seen in Figure 2 above. In order to achieve
optimum solar gain, the panels will be oriented in east-west rows with panels facing towards the
south.
The maximum height of the solar arrays will be approximately 3 metres from ground level to the top
of the back of the panel frame. The lowest edge of the panels will be raised above ground by at least

4.1.1 Calculating capacity
The following clarification takes account of National Policy Statement EN-3 and the Burnhope solar
farm judgement (Ian Galloway, R (on the application of) v Durham County Council [2024]. EWHC 367
(Admin))), both relating to how the capacity of a solar farm should be expressed.
The solar site capacity is up to 27.15MW. This is based on the combined capacity of all panels. The
site also contains 139 solar inverters which are used for converting the DC electricity produced by
the solar panels into AC power for export to the grid. As is normal for solar farm application, the final
specification of solar panels and type and combination of inverters may change due to technology
available at the time of construction with factors such as price, performance and availability
determining the final product specification. Nonetheless, the combined capacity of the solar panels
will not exceed 27.15MW. As such, it is clear that the application is not above the Nationally
Significant Infrastructure Project threshold for a solar project.
4.2 Inverters and transformers
The development includes solar inverters, which will convert the direct current (DC) electricity
output from the solar arrays into usable alternating current (AC) power for the electricity
distribution network.
There will be approximately 139 inverter cabins in total, though this may change during detailed
design once the choice of equipment is confirmed, and they will be located close to the internal
access track, to the southwest and centre of the site.
Transformers will be installed for each inverter to step up the low voltage electricity produced at the
site to high voltage for efficient transportation around the site and to the grid connection point. The
transformers are housed within the inverter cabins.

5.0 Background to the Proposals
5.1 Pre-application discussions
Pre-application advice was sought from WBC in September 2024 and written response received 27th
January 2025 (LPA ref: PRE/2024/00077). The scheme has evolved since the pre-application scheme
in response to LPA comments, technical reports and consultation with key stakeholders.
The LPA feedback was primarily concerned with the site’s location within the Warrington Green Belt.
The council views the proposal as ‘inappropriate development’ and therefore demonstration of
‘Very Special Circumstances’ (VSC) is required as set out in the NPPF for the project to be acceptable
in principle. However, the council also acknowledged the contribution the project would make
towards the delivery of renewable energy and national targets.
The Council also raised concerns about the loss of agricultural land, impacts of the proposal on visual
amenity and dwellings to the south, relationship to green infrastructure in the context of the PROW,
ecological considerations, heritage impact and highway matters.
Since the pre-application response was received, the project team has worked collaboratively to
address the Council’s comments. Details on how these requirements are met are provided in the
Planning Assessment section of this statement.
5.2 Environmental Impact Assessment
The relevant EIA Regulations are set out in the Town and Country Planning (Environmental Impact
Assessment) (England and Wales) Regulations 2011 (EIA Regs) and The Town and Country Planning
(Environmental Impact Assessment) (Amendment) Regulations 2017.
Schedule 1 of the EIA Regs includes a list of developments for which an EIA is mandatory. Schedule 2
of the EIA Regs includes a list of developments for which an EIA may be required with reference to
the screening criteria included in Schedule 3 of the Regulations.
The proposed development is considered to fall under point 3(a) of Column 1 of Schedule 2, which
relates to industrial installations for the production of electricity, steam and hot water (unless
included in Schedule 1).
A screening opinion was sought from WBC because, although the site is not in a “sensitive area” as
defined by the Regulations, the site area is over the 0.5 hectare relevant threshold under Column 2
of Schedule 2.
WBC confirmed on 11th March 2025 that an Environmental Statement was not required (LPA ref:
2025/00184/SCR).

8.2.1 Policy context
Chapter 13 of the NPPF clearly sets out the principles of development within the Green Belt.
Paragraph 143 outlines the five purposes of the green belt:
a) To check the unrestricted sprawl of large built-up areas.
b) To prevent neighbouring towns merging into each other.
c) To assist in safeguarding the countryside from encroachment.
d) To preserve the setting and special character of historic towns.
e) To assist in urban regeneration, by encouraging the recycling of derelict and other urban land.
Based on these purposes, any proposal for development within the Green Belt is considered
inappropriate (with a few exceptions set out in Para 154) and should only be approved in ‘very
special circumstances’ (VSC). Additionally, Para 160 states that renewable energy projects located
within the Green Belt “will comprise inappropriate development” and requires developers to
demonstrate VSCs. Local Plan Policy GB1 also notes that “planning permission will not be granted for
inappropriate development, except in ‘very special circumstances”.
Recent amendments to the NPPF also allow development on ‘Grey Belt’. This is defined as land that
does not ‘strongly contribute’ to the purposes of (a) (b) or (d) of the Green Belt – sprawl,
coalescence or setting of historic towns (Para 143, 155 and fn.). Subject to other matters identified
in paragraph 155, proposals on land that is considered Grey Belt, are not considered inappropriate
development and therefore VSC do not apply. This amendment represents a significant shift in
national policy and application of the policy and while relevant case law is still emerging, several
planning appeals have added clarity to how Inspectors are applying it.
The application site has considered this policy change and emerging case law3. As set out below in
Section 8.2.2 and in the Landscape and Visual Impact Assessment (Section 10 Green Belt

b or d. However, while a case could be made that site is considered Grey Belt, this application has
focused on establishing the case for VSC at this point in time as the more defined route through

some instances, an exception test may also be required (Para 177).

report identifies the flood risk classification of the site; that solar panels and associated
infrastructure are all located within Flood Zone 1; and only fences and CCTV will be located within

development is considered ‘Essential Infrastructure’ and therefore the design has sought to steer
development to areas on the site that are at low risk of flooding. Only some fences and CCTV are
within Flood Zone 2 & 3. These are integral to the proposal and cannot be located elsewhere in a
sequentially preferable location. Furthermore, they are considered ‘water compatible development’

operation during times of flooding, which is the case as only the fences and CCTV are located within
this area’.

rate is not increased above 2l/s. This will be achieved by ensuring access tracks are unpaved and
swales are provided at appropriate locations; the small hardstands needed for equipment will be

SUDs system is proposed that includes soakaways and infiltration drainage. The final design can be
secured via condition.

in Flood Zone 1 – the lowest probability of flooding. Only fencing and CCTV – defined as ‘water

ensure the proposal will not increase flood risk away from the site. Therefore, the proposal is

OUR PROPOSALS
If consented and installed, the solar farm will have a capacity of
up to 27MW and will be operational for up to 40 years. After that
time, all of the installations will be removed and the land restored
to how it was before, but with improved soil health and
biodiversity due to being left fallow. The power generated will be
exported to the grid via an underground cable route connecting
to the adjacent substation.
The solar panels will be installed in rows, which run east to west
through the site. These rows will be tilted towards the south to
capture the maximum amount of solar irradiation possible. The
total height of the panels will be no more than 3m above the
ground with a gap of more than 0.8m above the ground at its
minimum.
Small electric cabins will be located amongst the panels and
these will be accessed by a network of crushed stone track which
will run through the site. All of the panels will be surrounded by
security fencing to protect the equipment from large animals
entering the site. CCTV cameras will be located periodically
around the site perimeter for security and face inwards.
The development will also involve additional landscaping
including hedgerow planting and improved biodiversity
management. Land between and beneath the panels can be
used for biodiversity enhancements if desired. The site is
currently agricultural land with low ecological value, however
with the implementation of a biodiversity management plan the
site will be significantly enhanced for biodiversity by creating a
range of new habitats, offering food and shelter to wildlife.
